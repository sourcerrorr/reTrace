Role You are a senior product designer and front-end engineer. Build a desktop web application called reTrace — a calm, accessible, at-home rehabilitation tool for stroke survivors doing upper-limb therapy. Output responsive HTML/CSS/JS (Tailwind or vanilla, no framework required). The webcam and hand tracking are already handled — you are designing and building the UI shell and all overlays around them. Product in one paragraph The user sits in front of their laptop. The webcam feed fills the main area, mirrored horizontally, with a drawing canvas overlaid on top. The user moves their hand in the air; their index fingertip acts as a pen. They perform therapy exercises by tracing shapes or reaching for on-screen targets. reTrace is free, browser-only, requires no account, and never uploads video. Everything happens locally. This privacy promise must be visible in the UI. Audience & tone Primary user: stroke survivor, 55–80 years old, doing self-directed upper-limb therapy at home, possibly with a caregiver nearby. Tone: calm, warm, encouraging. Never clinical, never childishly gamified, never punitive. Praise before correction. Language at ~6th-grade reading level. Own the machine's failures ("I can't see your hand" — never "you are out of frame"). Accessibility floor (non-negotiable) Body text ≥ 18 px, headlines ≥ 32 px, in-session cues ≥ 28 px. Touch/click targets ≥ 56 px; primary session buttons 64–72 px. Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 body, ≥ 7:1 during exercises. Color always paired with icon and text. Full keyboard operability. Focus rings 3 px, 2 px offset. Space = pause, Esc = exit, Enter = confirm. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. No autoplay animations. No time pressure. No dropdowns anywhere — every choice is a big tappable card. What NOT to do No login, sign-up, account, pricing, upgrade, or share buttons anywhere. No small text. Ever. If it doesn't fit at 18 px, it doesn't belong. No dropdowns, sliders, or free-text inputs anywhere in the therapy flows. No red — use terracotta for danger and clay for missed days. No leaderboards, streak-shaming, percentiles, letter grades, or badges shaped like trophies/medals. No emoji, no cartoon mascots, no confetti. No timers ticking during an exercise — do not add urgency the user did not ask for. No blue medical clip-art. No stethoscope icons. No sterile pure white.
